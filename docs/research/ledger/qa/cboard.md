# Coordinator QA: Cboard

Decision: add the browser AAC application under UNICEF's public-international-organization sponsorship, not as sovereign-state software or as solely authored by UNICEF.

- Government/public-international provenance: UNICEF's [official FAQ](https://www.unicef.org/about/frequently-asked-questions) states that it is a permanent part of the UN system and describes the Executive Board's intergovernmental oversight; the [UN establishment record](https://www.unicef.org/executiveboard/documents/establishment-international-childrens-emergency-fund-1946) documents its General Assembly origin. UNICEF's [Office of Innovation cohort page](https://www.unicef.org/innovation/venturefund/unicef-accessibility-portfolio-aac) identifies CIREHA/Cboard as selected for the Innovation Fund AAC cohort, and its [CIREHA graduate story](https://www.unicef.org/innovation/stories/unicef-innovation-fund-graduate-cireha) describes the Fund's investment and support for product development. This is product-specific public-international funding; CIREHA is the Argentine developer.
- Public source and license: the [cboard-org/cboard application repository](https://github.com/cboard-org/cboard) is public; its [root license](https://github.com/cboard-org/cboard/blob/master/LICENSE.txt) and README grant GPL-3.0-only to the browser application code. The separately licensed Mulberry/ARASAAC symbol assets and sibling cboard-api backend are not folded into that grant.
- Year and use: the initial public source commit (2017-08-03) includes an explicit GPLv3 statement ([commit](https://github.com/cboard-org/cboard/commit/22dc5623601b43c539be11d368fe0353c10b3933)). UNICEF documents Cboard pilots with children and professionals in the Balkans in its [product use story](https://www.unicef.org/innovation/stories/giving-every-child-voice-aac-technology); the official website continues to offer the application. The record geography is global for the sponsor while identifying CIREHA in Argentina.
