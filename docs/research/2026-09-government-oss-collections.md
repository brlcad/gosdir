# Government open-source collections: ingestion survey

Research date: 22 September 2026. This is a discovery and sizing report, not a bulk import.

Publication follow-up: the [first publication audit](2026-09-government-oss-agent-review.md) records 25 earlier published projects. The 28 September [candidate ledger](ledger/README.md) carries 10 of those earlier decisions into this 36-project shortlist and adds fresh individual reviews for the other 26. It currently records **30 publishable decisions and six holds** for that shortlist. The thousands of other repository leads have not been individually reviewed; use the ledger's generated summary for current counts.

## Answer

At the 22 September survey, GOSDIR had 32 software records and this report proposed **36 additional project identities** for closer review. The subsequent [freshness and notability screen](2026-09-government-oss-intake-baseline.md) advanced 33 and held three; that was not a final four-check publication decision. The current ledger status is **30 publishable and six held** shortlist projects, combining 10 earlier publication decisions and 26 new individual reviews. Three of the new reviews found license-scope or first-licensed-year uncertainties. This is a bounded shortlist, not an estimate of eligible products in the collections.

The source inventories expose many thousands of additional repository leads. The strongest near-term international pool is the [Swiss Federal Chancellery OSS Catalogue](https://www.opensource.admin.ch/en/): 74 distinct, described, OSI-licensed, stable/beta standalone repository URLs after deduplication, none a direct match to GOSDIR or the 17-source govoss union. Twenty-one have “example” in their names; a name screen excluding examples, documentation, GitOps and catalog repos leaves about 50 application-component URLs. Several components still belong to the same product, so even 50 is a review queue rather than a product forecast. DOE CODE offers much larger U.S. reach, with 4,960 distinct stated repository URLs after selecting its public-repository open-source records with a named license; that is a queue, not 4,960 verified products.

The best feeds to review first are [DOE CODE](https://www.osti.gov/doecode/), the [Swiss catalogue](https://www.opensource.admin.ch/en/), [Dutch OSS Register](https://oss.developer.overheid.nl/), [NASA's approved open-source catalog](https://github.com/nasa/Open-Source-Catalog), [Colorado OpenCDSS](https://opencdss.state.co.us/opencdss/), and focused first-party product pages such as [Singapore Open Government Products](https://www.open.gov.sg/for-developers/open-source/). The [govoss union catalog](https://govoss-catalog.vercel.app/) and [OSOR repository directory](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/open-source-observatory-osor/oss-repositories) are excellent discovery maps, but recommendation/use-only entries do not establish government provenance.

## Counting rule

A GOSDIR software record requires documented government creation, commissioning, funding, sponsorship or stewardship; public canonical source; an OSI-approved license or clear public-domain basis; and primary evidence. See the [site methodology](../../app/methodology/page.tsx) and [catalog contribution rules](../../lib/catalog/README.md). The existing checker validates shape and duplicate repository URLs. It does not prove license, liveness or sponsor.

Counts below have distinct units. Raw source rows may include duplicates. A repository URL is not necessarily a reusable software product. A URL is called directly new after lowercasing host/path and removing trailing slash and .git, then comparing against GOSDIR and, where noted, govoss. Redirects, mirrors, unlabeled forks and multiple repos for one product still need editorial deduplication. Every number is a 22 September 2026 snapshot unless a source gives an older data date.

## Search tags and manually curated collections

- [GitHub’s government topic](https://github.com/topics/government) displayed 2,051 repositories on review. Its first page mixes actual government projects with vendors, nonprofits, datasets and documentation. The [publiccode topic](https://github.com/topics/publiccode) showed only 18, mostly metadata tools. Topics are discovery signals, never sponsor proof.
- [GitHub Government’s organization directory](https://government.github.com/community/) and [Government apps collection](https://github.com/collections/government) reveal agency accounts and hand-picked examples. The collection also contains guidance and nongovernment civic projects.
- The European Commission’s [OSOR repository directory](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/open-source-observatory-osor/oss-repositories) points to national, regional and local catalogs. Its [EU OSS Catalogue](https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/eu-oss-catalogue/solutions) federates sources and contains generic upstream OSS, so member catalogs offer clearer provenance and fewer duplicates.
- [Singapore OGP’s official list](https://www.open.gov.sg/for-developers/open-source/) names six government products and links code. FormSG is already in GOSDIR; GoGovSG and MockPass below have eligible terms. Other entries need license review.
- [GSA’s OSS examples](https://open.gsa.gov/oss-examples/) and [GOV.UK Pay’s source-components page](https://docs.payments.service.gov.uk/contribute/) are small, high-precision government curations. Multiple components of one service should usually map to one product record.
- The [Colorado OpenCDSS product directory](https://opencdss.state.co.us/opencdss/) lists eight state-funded water-planning tools or components, including the four checked below. It provides [funding](https://opencdss.state.co.us/opencdss/workflow/) and [licensing](https://opencdss.state.co.us/opencdss/licensing/) context; remaining components need product/repo checks.
- The [Japan Digital Agency’s official GitHub organization](https://github.com/digital-go-jp) held 16 public repositories on review, a small bounded lead.

## Limits and next work

1. **Recommendation is not provenance.** France SILL, Munich third-party deployments, Digital Public Goods, OSOR and EU OSS listings include software governments can use but did not necessarily create or materially sponsor.
2. **Code inventories are not product inventories.** A service may have several backends, SDKs, tests, deployment recipes and archived versions. Conversely, GOSDIR currently has two organization-URL software records (cloud.gov and code.mil), so exact-repo URL dedup alone misses nested product relationships.
3. **License metadata needs confirmation.** NIST code.json points every row at one generic NIST grant, while EPA's generator infers missing data. A deterministic diagnostic sample of 10 GitHub repo slugs per NASA/NIST/EPA source (sort URLs, then Python random.Random(419).sample(slugs, 10)) found GitHub-detected license files in 7/10 NASA, 7/10 NIST and 3/10 EPA repos; one NASA repo was unavailable, one NASA and three NIST repos archived. This small sample tests data quality, **not** population acceptance rate. A similarly seeded 15-project USGS sample returned 15 GitLab license entries, including some content or “other” licenses.
4. **Freshness and rights matter.** The French inventory says it stopped refreshing in April 2025; many NASA entries are older. Re-resolve redirects. The [govoss compilation is CC BY 4.0 while individual entries retain upstream terms](https://github.com/sarapis/govoss-catalog#licence); its records should lead back to primary evidence. The [French inventory has Licence Ouverte v2](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/inventaire-des-codes-sources-de-logiciels-publies-par-des-organismes-publics), and the [UK mapping is MIT](https://publicsectorcodebyorg.co.uk/data/).

Draft only projects with a current `publishable` ledger decision, checking current default branch, license, agency relation, official URL and status at publication; keep all six shortlist holds out of the catalog. Continue disjoint source-specific batches across Swiss, Dutch, French, DOE and other collections.
